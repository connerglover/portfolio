1.1.6 identified parts by building them and watching what they did. 1.1.7 identifies them from the manufacturer's own document instead, which is the version that scales — you cannot breadboard every part you will ever meet, but you can read the datasheet for any of them. Four deliverables: print the useful pages of five datasheets, name five unfamiliar part numbers, match gate symbols to 74LS devices, and expand five package-style abbreviations.

The five datasheets are the parts the rest of Unit 1 runs on — 74LS04 hex inverter, 74LS08 quad 2-input AND, 74LS32 quad 2-input OR, 74LS74 dual positive-edge-triggered D flip-flop with preset and clear, and the LM555 timer — all pulled from TI. Only pages 1 and 2 get printed, and which two it is carries the point of the exercise. Page 1 carries the connection diagram: pin numbering, which gate each pin belongs to, and where V_CC and GND land on the package, so it is what you wire from. Page 2 carries the function table: input-to-output behaviour, including the clock, preset and clear conditions on the sequential parts, so it is what you predict from. Ten pages covers all five parts, and a datasheet's remaining thirty pages of switching characteristics and thermal data are not needed to build anything on a breadboard.

The five unfamiliar parts split into four gates and one display. DM74LS00 is a quad 2-input NAND and DM74LS75 a 4-bit bistable latch — transparent, so its outputs follow the inputs while the enable is asserted and hold when it is not — both Fairchild Semiconductor. SN74LS02 is a quad 2-input NOR and SN74LS86 a quad 2-input XOR, both Texas Instruments. MAN6760 is the odd one out: a 0.56-inch single-digit seven-segment LED display, common anode, red, from Fairchild. The pattern in the part numbers is worth more than any single answer. SN is Texas Instruments and DM is Fairchild, but the prefix only names the house that packaged the part; the digits after it — 74LS00, 74LS86 — are what identify the logic function, and the same die ships under both. That is why a bill of materials calling for a 74LS00 is satisfied by either.

Matching gate symbols to parts is three questions asked in order, and the order matters because each one narrows the field. The back of the symbol gives the family: a flat back is the AND family, a curved shield back is the OR family. The input line count separates parts within that family — three inputs and no bubble is a 3-input AND, the 74LS11, while four inputs and no bubble is a 4-input AND, the 74LS21. A bubble on the output inverts it, which turns each of those into its N-form: 74LS10 for the 3-input NAND and 74LS20 for the 4-input NAND. The fifth symbol is the shield back with three inputs and a bubble, so OR family, three inputs, inverted — a 3-input NOR, the 74LS27. Nothing here needs memorising as a table; shape, count, bubble reaches the right part every time.

The package styles are the same silicon in five different bodies, and the split that organises them is through-hole against surface mount. DIP is the Dual In-line Package, the one every part in this unit arrives in, with two rows of leads on a 2.54 mm pitch that push straight into a breadboard. SOIC is the Small Outline Integrated Circuit, the same pin count at half the pitch with gull-wing leads that solder onto a pad rather than through a hole. QFP is the Quad Flat Package, which puts leads on all four sides so the pin count grows with the perimeter instead of the length. PLCC is the Plastic Leaded Chip Carrier, identifiable by its chamfered corner and by J-leads that curl back under the body. BGA is the Ball Grid Array, which gives up leads entirely for a grid of solder balls on the underside — the highest pin density of the five, and the only one you cannot inspect or rework by eye.

Ten printed pages, three tables. It is the least hands-on activity in the unit and the one the next two depend on most: the Random Number Generator in 1.1.8 and 1.1.9 gets soldered rather than simulated, and a soldered board offers no probe to drag onto a node when a pin turns out to be the wrong one. The connection diagram and function table in the deck are drawn rather than photographed, from the TI pinouts and the 74LS74A truth table.
